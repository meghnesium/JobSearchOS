# Universal style rules

These rules apply to every letter, bio, outreach message and resume header any JobSearchOS skill writes. They are not personal. The user's own `profile/voice.md` ADDS to these lists (extra banned words, phrase bank, tone). It never removes anything from them.

Spirit over letter, but the hard rules are absolute: one occurrence fails the draft.

## Hard rules

1. **Zero em dashes.** Use commas, periods, colons, semicolons or parentheses. Also avoid the en dash used as a pause.
2. **Zero negate-then-reframe constructions.** Any sentence that negates one framing and then asserts a corrected one. Examples: "It's not X, it's Y." "Not just X but Y." "Less X, more Y." "Rather than X, Y." "You don't need X, you need Y." "While X might seem right, Y is actually..." "Sure, X works. But Y is where the real..." Fix: delete everything before the positive claim and state what it is.
3. **Zero dead AI words:** delve, realm, harness, unlock, paradigm, leverage, showcase, serve as, stand as, represents, marks a, features, boasts, highlight, emphasize, innovative, game-changer, breakthrough, seamless, optimize, empower, transform, synergy, disruptive. Use a plain verb instead.
4. **Zero dead phrases:** "In today's...", "Let's dive in", "At the end of the day", "Moving forward", "It's important to note that", "In order to", "Furthermore", "Additionally", "Moreover", "That said", "On top of that".
5. **Zero cover-letter cliches:** "I'm passionate about", "I'm excited about the opportunity", "I came across your job posting", "I believe my skills align", "As a seasoned [title]", "I would love the chance to", "Thank you for considering", "I am writing to apply".
6. **Zero meta-commentary:** "In this section I will...", "Let me walk you through...", "Here's a comprehensive overview...". Say the thing. Do not announce that you are about to say it. Never announce your method; section labels already do that.
7. **Never restate the job ad back at the company.** Never list tools or software fluency when the role is senior or above.
8. **Never open on a problem with their product.** A stranger who opens with what is wrong with the product reads as unsolicited consulting. Openings end on a positive: an outcome, current work, a reason.
9. **Never name a gap in their team.** Describe what you bring and let them connect it. (Gaps in the user's own background belong on the Decision Card, never in the letter.)
10. **Every number needs a source.** It must trace to a file in `profile/pillars/`. If a number is not there, leave it out. A missing number beats a vague or invented one.
11. **Bold is banned,** with one exception: the closing clause of `resolution` (structured letters) is wrapped in `<strong>`. Nowhere else.

## Strong tendencies (roughly four times in five)

- Short paragraphs: one or two sentences, three at most.
- Contractions. Direct address (I, you). Active voice.
- Specific nouns, numbers and concrete examples over abstractions.
- Vary sentence length. Short punchy lines next to longer ones.
- Start a paragraph with And, But, So when it sounds like speech.
- Parenthetical asides for honest reactions.
- Commit to a stance. State facts and let the reader judge significance.
- Avoid the rule of three. Use two things or four.
- Stop when the point is made. No summary of what was just said.

## Attach the caveat

Attach the caveat to your own numbers yourself, in the same sentence or the next. Admitting the limit before the reader finds it is the strongest credibility move available. Pull the caveat from the pillar file's `Numbers` and `Honest limit` sections; do not invent one.

## The Acme test

Swap the company name for "Acme" and reread. If the letter still makes sense, the opening is generic. Rewrite it around something only this company has. Run this on the opening, the impact card and the close.

## The abstraction test

Paste a sentence into another candidate's letter. If it survives there unchanged ("I brought systems thinking to a fragmented platform"), delete it and name the specific artifact, decision or number instead. Every beat needs a noun only the user could have written.

## Temperature

- **Measured:** enterprise, regulated, formal ad. Two numbers with caveats attached. Signals "I will not break your system".
- **Bold:** startup, opinionated culture, a manifesto on the careers page. One number stated casually, one visible hedge, willing to be wrong out loud.
- **Default:** measured plus exactly one bold sentence.

Full bold is a choice made on evidence, not on mood. Read the user's default from `profile/voice.md` and record the choice in the dossier `tone` field.

## Extending the lists

Read `profile/voice.md` for additional banned words, banned phrases and a phrase bank. Treat them as extra entries in rules 3 to 5.

## Adapting to the user's role

Some wording in the skills comes from product and design work: "challenge", "screen", "surface", "interface". Read them through `identity.role_title`. For a product manager the challenge may be a funnel, a workflow or a pricing page. For an engineer it may be a system, an incident or an architecture decision. For marketing it may be a campaign or a conversion path. The rules do not change: one real, sourced problem they have not solved, described not judged, that the user has receipts for.
