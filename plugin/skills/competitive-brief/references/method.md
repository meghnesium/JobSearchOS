# Competitive brief

5 minutes inside the 30-minute research clock in the fast version, about 15 minutes standalone. Not a market report. One question: **is the challenge I am about to name the thing they are already losing on?** That is the difference between a letter that reads as insightful and one that reads as naive.

## Find the 3 competitors (2 min)

Fastest paths, in order:

1. Their own comparison pages. Search `[company] vs`. If they publish them, they have told you who they fear.
2. The "compared to" section on G2 or Capterra.
3. The job ad, which sometimes names the category and so the incumbents.
4. Their funding announcement. Press coverage names the rivals.

Pick one incumbent, one direct rival, one adjacent threat (a tool from a neighbouring category eating the same budget). If you can only find one, that is a finding: the category is new, or they compete against spreadsheets and internal tools. Competing against a spreadsheet is a good problem.

## The three lines (3 min)

**1. Their one real differentiator.** Not the marketing claim. The thing structurally hard to copy: proprietary data, a distribution channel, a regulatory position, deep integration, or genuinely better UX. If the honest answer is "nothing structural, they win on sales," write that. It tells you the function is either undervalued there or about to become the strategy.

**2. Their one real gap.** Where a rival is visibly better. Check competitor changelogs and the 3-star reviews of both.

**3. Product or distribution.** Are they winning on the product, or on getting in front of people? This call changes the whole letter.

- Winning on product: write about depth, systems, the hard moment.
- Winning on distribution with a weaker product: write about the gap between the promise sales makes and what the product delivers. Often the strongest angle.
- Losing on both: probably skip, unless the ad reads like they know it.

## The one thing that makes this worth 5 minutes

Look for a **category convention they have broken or failed to adopt.** If everyone else has a command palette and they do not, that is either a deliberate stance or a blind spot. Either way it is a sentence no other applicant will have written.

> "Everyone in this category ships saved views. You do not, and your planning view resets its filters on navigation. I am curious whether that is a deliberate call."

## What to write in the dossier

```markdown
## Competitive brief

**Competitors:** [incumbent] / [direct] / [adjacent]

**Their real differentiator:** [structural, not marketing]
**Their real gap:** [where a rival is visibly better]
**Winning on:** product / distribution / neither

**Convention broken or missed:** [one, or "none found"]

**Read on the challenge:** already solved by rivals / unsolved by everyone / they are the ones behind on it
```

The last field feeds check 4 of the Squeeze quality test.

## Two failure modes

**Do not teach them their own market.** Use the finding to sharpen your observation, then let it stay implicit.

- Bad: "As you know, Competitor X is gaining share with its self-serve motion."
- Good: "Demo-only makes sense while the model is the sell. It also means a new user's first screen is one someone else configured for them."

**Do not name a competitor unless you are saying something generous about your target.** Naming a rival critically reads as tactless. Naming the category convention is safe.
