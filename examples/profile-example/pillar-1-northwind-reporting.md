# Pillar 1: Executive reporting suite

## Use when

Enterprise, regulated or data-heavy products where the people deciding are senior and hard to reach.

## One-line claim

I designed the reporting suite that Northwind's executive customers used to run their quarterly reviews, and decided what it would refuse to show.

## Numbers (all defensible)

- 6 of 9 enterprise customers cited the reporting suite in renewal calls. Method: renewal call notes tagged by the account team. Caveat: self-reported by customers, not a controlled comparison.
- 11 of 15 roadmap items in one planning cycle were reporting work. Method: the planning snapshot from that quarter. Caveat: it measures priority, not usage.
- Design cycle per new report fell from 6 sessions to 3. Method: my own session log over the last four reports. Caveat: small sample, same team throughout.

### Needs a source (not usable in letters yet)

- "Customers love it" (no survey behind it)

## The hardest call

I cut the comparison table the head of sales asked for. Tables made the room read numbers instead of arguing about what the numbers meant. We shipped one chart per decision instead.

## The systems insight

One narrative structure (question, data, description, insight) scaled across every report and became the design language.

## Outlived my involvement

Two reports shipped after I moved teams, built by other designers using the same structure without my review.

## Honest limit

Most of it shipped as prototypes into sales demos. The numbers measure sales and roadmap influence, not production usage.

## Which numbers can close a resolution

"3 sessions instead of 6 per report, from my own log over four reports." Behaviour change, small sample, said as such.

## Best lines

> The platform extracted the data. People built the conviction.
